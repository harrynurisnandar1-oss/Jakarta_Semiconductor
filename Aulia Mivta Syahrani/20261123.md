Lesson Learned

Active Power Delivered: Active power transmitted from the source to the load.

Active Power Received: Active power returned from the load to the source.

<img width="231" height="242" alt="Screenshot 2026-11-23 092438" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/912c2749-d13f-4c94-8c52-7402a3394d71" />

Quadrant I

W (+), VAR (+)

Active Delivered + Reactive Delivered

- Active power flows from the source to the load.

- Reactive power also flows from the source to the load.

- Load characteristic: Inductive.

- Examples: induction motors, transformers, and coils.

   Phase angle: 0° to 90°.


Quadrant II

W (−), VAR (+)

Active Received + Reactive Delivered

- Active power flows back from the load to the source.

- Reactive power flows from the source to the load.
  
- Characteristics: negative active power and positive reactive power.

- Can occur when a system exports active power while still requiring inductive reactive power.

  Phase angle: 90° to 180°.


Quadrant III

W (−), VAR (−)

Active Received + Reactive Received

- Active power flows back from the load toward the source.

- Reactive power also flows back toward the source.

- Load characteristic: Capacitive.

- Can occur in a system that exports active power and exhibits capacitive characteristics.

  Phase angle: 180° to 270°.


Quadrant IV

W (+), VAR (−)

Active Delivered + Reactive Received

- Active power flows from the source toward the load.

- Reactive power flows back from the load toward the source.

- Load characteristic: Capacitive.

- Example: a load with capacitor compensation.

  Phase angle: 270° to 360°.
