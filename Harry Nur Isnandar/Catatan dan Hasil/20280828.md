# Theory

### Combining Batteries
Series Circuit (End-to-End): Connecting batteries in series increases the total supply voltage (the final voltage is the sum of all the battery voltages).

Parallel Circuit: Connecting positive terminals to positive and negative terminals to negative increases the current capacity (extends the operating time/battery life).

### Major Types of Damage

Short Circuit: Occurs when two conductors come into contact (e.g., crossed wires, insulation failure, or solder sparks). This results in an excessive current flow that can burn out components, blow a fuse, and drop the voltage at the short-circuit point to zero. 

Open Circuit: Occurs when a wire or component lead breaks, or when a component burns out or is damaged, resulting in extremely high resistance that stops the current from flowing.

### Ideal vs. Realistic Conditions

In theory (ideal), a short circuit in an ideal voltage source produces an infinite current.

In practice (realistic), voltage sources and conductors have internal resistance that limits the maximum current, although the value remains high enough to damage the circuit.

### Damage Detection & Protection

Detection: Short circuits are often indicated by a burning smell or overheated components.

Prevention: Use protective devices such as fuses, circuit breakers, and transient voltage suppressors. These devices intentionally open the circuit when they detect an excessive current to limit damage.

### Major Types of Circuit Failures

Short Circuit: Occurs when two conductors come into contact (e.g., crossed wires, insulation failure, or solder sparks). This results in an excessive current flow that can burn out components, blow a fuse, and reduce the voltage at the short-circuit point to zero.

Open Circuit: Occurs when a wire or component lead breaks, or when a component burns out or is damaged, resulting in extremely high resistance that stops the current from flowing.

### Charge- Based Model of Current Through a Capacitor.
Current appears to flow because a change in charge on one plate triggers the release of an equal amount of charge on the other plate, even though no electrons cross the gap between the plates.

Ideal Capacitor: Acts as an open circuit for a constant direct current, but triggers an infinite current surge if there is a sudden change in voltage.

Realistic Capacitor: Internal resistance limits the initial current surge so it is not infinite; the current then decays as the charge builds up.

Inductor Analogy: A very heavy water wheel (with inertia) inside a pipe.

Water Begins to Flow: The heavy wheel resists the initial flow (prevents a sudden surge in current).

Stable Water Flow: The wheel spins fully and no longer obstructs the water (functions like a regular wire in a DC circuit).

Water Suddenly Shut Off: The wheel continues to spin and push the water, creating a high-pressure surge (prevents the current from stopping suddenly/triggers a voltage spike).

###  Modeling Complex Circuits

Modeling Complex Circuits: Kirchhoff’s laws apply to all types of circuits (both linear and nonlinear). Linear circuits have a response that is proportional to the input signal.

Characteristics of Linear Components:

Resistor: Doubling the voltage doubles the current.

Capacitor: Doubling the frequency of the voltage doubles the current.

Inductor: Doubling the voltage frequency will halve the current.

Mathematical Equations for Circuits:

Linear DC Circuits: Described using linear algebraic equations.

Linear Time-Dependent Circuits: Described using linear differential equations (caused by sinusoidal sources or transient conditions when a DC source is turned on or off).

### Circuits with Sinusoidal Sources and Analyzing Sinusoidal Circuits with Complex Impedances

Sinusoidal Sources & Phasors

All voltages and currents in a linear AC circuit have the same frequency as the source, differing only in amplitude and phase angle.
Signals are converted to phasor form to avoid complex mathematical calculations.

Complex Impedance

This is the total resistance in an AC circuit, consisting of real resistance (which generates heat) and reactive resistance (which stores energy).
In a resistor, voltage and current flow in phase; in an inductor, voltage leads the current; whereas in a capacitor, voltage lags the current.

Circuit Analysis

All DC circuit laws (Ohm’s Law, Kirchhoff’s Laws, voltage divider rule, and node/loop analysis) still apply to AC circuits by replacing ordinary resistance with complex impedance.
Circuits are solved by converting the sources to phasors, calculating the impedances of the components, solving the circuit equations, and then converting the results back to waveforms.
